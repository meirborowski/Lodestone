# 0021 - Physics: Jolt Bodies Derived from Components, Saved with the Simulation

## Status
Accepted

## Context
Milestone 7 adds rigid bodies, colliders, triggers, raycasts, collision and trigger events, and a character controller driven by input commands, with [Jolt Physics](https://github.com/jrouwe/JoltPhysics) stepped once per simulation tick. Its acceptance test is rollback: restoring a saved state and re-simulating the same input commands reproduces the same physics state on the same machine ([Decision 0012](0012-simulation-and-rollback.md)).

[Prediction](../Features/Networking.md#prediction) asked for its scope to be confirmed before this milestone, since the character controller and physics state are built around it. Jolt shapes the rest: its simulation is deterministic when the same binary makes the same API calls in the same order; `SaveState` records only what a step changes (positions, velocities, sleep state, contacts), and `RestoreState` needs the same bodies, with the same IDs, to exist already; contact callbacks come from several threads in no particular order; and a body that falls asleep reports its contacts as removed.

## Decision
- **Prediction scope, confirmed as documented** - clients predict only the locally controlled character's movement: the `CharacterController` component, driven by the input commands of its player. Reconciliation restores the server's state for a tick and re-simulates the commands the server hasn't processed. Everything else - other bodies, other players, scripted gameplay - is authoritative on the server and reaches clients through replication and interpolation; scripts are never re-simulated. So the controller's movement depends only on its components, the world's colliders and input commands, never on scripts, and everything prediction restores (its transform, velocity and whether it's grounded) is in replicated component fields
- **Components are the authored truth; Jolt holds the simulated state** - `RigidBody` (static, kinematic or dynamic; mass, friction, restitution, damping, gravity factor, continuous collision, sleeping, trigger, and its linear and angular velocity), the colliders `BoxCollider`, `SphereCollider`, `CapsuleCollider` and `MeshCollider` (a model asset's mesh, as a triangle mesh or its convex hull), `CharacterController`, and `PhysicsSettings` (gravity, one per scene). Each tick, `PhysicsWorld` brings Jolt in line with the components - creating, removing and updating bodies, moving static and kinematic bodies to their entities, and teleporting dynamic bodies and characters whose transforms or velocities were changed by something other than physics - then steps, then writes dynamic bodies' and characters' transforms and velocities back. "Changed by something else" means different from what physics last wrote, so edits through the inspector, MCP or scripts all take effect without notifying physics
- **Which body a collider belongs to** - an entity's colliders belong to its nearest ancestor-or-self with a `RigidBody`; colliders below a `CharacterController` are ignored (it's a capsule of its own); colliders with neither above them are a static body of their own entity. Colliders on descendants make a compound shape, placed by the local transforms between them and the body - not by world transforms, whose rounding would reshape the body a little every time it moved. The world scale of each collider's entity is baked into its shape. A triangle mesh can't be dynamic: a dynamic body uses a mesh's convex hull, and logs a warning if asked for the triangles
- **Triggers are sensor bodies** - a `RigidBody` with `IsTrigger` detects bodies and characters overlapping it without colliding. Layers keep triggers from testing against static geometry and each other: static, moving, trigger and character layers, with static, moving and trigger bodies in broad-phase layers of their own
- **The character controller is Jolt's `CharacterVirtual`** - a capsule standing on the entity's position, kept upright, turned by the mouse and the right stick, moved by WASD, the arrow keys and the left stick relative to where it faces, jumping with Space or gamepad A when grounded, walking up steps and sticking to slopes up to its maximum. In the air it keeps its momentum, steered toward walking at its air acceleration. These bindings are fixed until named input actions exist. It has an inner kinematic body, so raycasts hit it, triggers detect it and fast bodies can't pass through it; other characters are handled by Jolt's character-vs-character collision
- **State saved with the simulation** - `Simulation` gains stateful systems, which save their own state beside the scene's snapshot and restore it after the scene. `PhysicsWorld` is one: its state is Jolt's full state (bodies, contacts) through a `StateRecorder`, every character's state, and its own bookkeeping - which body belongs to which entity, what it last wrote to each, which pairs are touching, and its body and character ID allocators. Restoring brings Jolt's set of bodies back in line with that bookkeeping and the restored components (recreating removed bodies with their old IDs, removing newer ones, reapplying settings), then restores Jolt's state into them
- **Deterministic IDs** - body and character IDs come from the world's own allocators (the lowest free index, with a sequence number that changes on reuse), saved with its state, and bodies are created with `CreateBodyWithID` - so re-simulating after a restore creates bodies with the IDs they had the first time, which Jolt needs for its state to apply
- **Events from contact pairs, sorted** - collision and trigger events are begin and end events between two entities, gathered after each step: pairs Jolt reports as added begin; a touching pair ends when Jolt says the bodies are no longer in contact, unless both are asleep (they still touch). Characters' contacts come from their own contact lists. Events are sorted, so they come out in the same order every run, and kept for the tick's later systems (scripts, in Milestone 8)
- **Raycasts** - the closest hit or every hit along a ray, with the entity, point, normal and distance, ties broken by body ID so the result is deterministic. Triggers are skipped unless asked for
- **Jolt configured for Lodestone** - built from `Jolt/Jolt.cmake` (its own project changes global flags and adds samples), linked privately by `LodestoneCore`, so Jolt stays an implementation detail of the physics module. Single precision; SSE4.2 on x86-64 (AVX2 would crash on CPUs without it), no FMA; no cross-platform determinism (the server is authoritative, so same-machine determinism is enough); asserts routed to the engine's in Debug and Release; the system allocator under sanitizers. Each world has its own job system - Jolt is deterministic regardless of thread count
- **The editor** - play mode creates a physics world for the play scene and adds it before animation; the viewport draws the selected entities' colliders; MCP gets `physics_raycast` (in play mode against the running world, while editing against a world built from the document) and `physics_events` (the latest collision and trigger events in play mode)

## Alternatives
- **Predicting every body** - pushing a crate would feel immediate, but every client would re-simulate the whole world on every snapshot, mispredict whenever another player touched something, and diverge wherever scripts move bodies, since they aren't re-simulated. Small sessions (2-16 players) with authoritative physics don't need it
- **Jolt as the source of truth, with a body handle in the scene** - simpler syncing, but edits through reflection (the inspector, MCP, replication) would bypass physics unless every path called into it, and the scene would no longer describe the world on its own
- **Body IDs in an internal component** - the mapping would be snapshotted with the scene, but the ID allocator's state must be saved anyway, and keeping the mapping with it keeps Jolt's details inside the physics module
- **Rebuilding the world from components on restore** - no state recording, but contacts and warm starting would be lost, so re-simulation would differ from the original run
- **Cross-platform determinism** - about 8% slower, and not needed while the server is authoritative

## Consequences
- Dynamic bodies move in world space: a dynamic body's transform is written relative to its parent, but moving the parent doesn't drag it along
- Static bodies follow their entities when moved (a teleport, which pushes nothing, though it wakes the bodies around it, before and after); moving platforms should be kinematic. Bodies around a body that's reshaped or removed are woken too, so nothing is left asleep in the air
- Colliders on animated joints stay at the joint's rest pose: poses are derived, not stored ([Decision 0020](0020-skeletal-animation.md))
- The character controller's bindings are fixed until the named input actions of [Input](../Features/Input.md) exist; scripts can't steer it until Milestone 8
- Collision filtering is by those four layers only; user-defined collision layers can be added later
