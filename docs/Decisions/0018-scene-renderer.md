# 0018 - The Scene Renderer: Forward PBR with a Prepass, CSM and PCSS, GTAO and Split-Sum IBL

## Status
Accepted

## Context
Milestone 5 needs a renderer with PBR materials, image-based lighting from HDRIs, soft shadows, good ambient occlusion and an HDR pipeline with tonemapping, sustaining 60 fps at 1080p on an RTX 3060-class GPU with every feature on. It renders through NVRHI on Vulkan 1.3 (see [Decision 0007](0007-rendering-backend.md)), its images are compared on lavapipe (see [Decision 0009](0009-lavapipe.md)), and the editor (viewport, screenshots, picking), the runtime and the benchmark all use it. The engine aims to be simple and straightforward, so the design favors few, well-understood techniques.

## Decision
- **The renderer reads a `RenderWorld`, not the ECS** - `ExtractRenderWorld()` (`Lodestone/Renderer/RenderWorld.h`) collects meshes, lights, the environment, settings and the primary camera from a scene in hierarchy order, with transforms interpolated between ticks (see [Decision 0012](0012-simulation-and-rollback.md)). The render components (`Scene/RenderComponents.h`) are plain data in Core, so the server and scripts can read them; the renderer is in Client
- **Forward shading over a depth and normal prepass** - shadow maps, then a prepass (depth and view-space normals), ambient occlusion from it, the lit opaque surfaces over the prepass's depth (each pixel shaded once), the sky behind them, transparent surfaces sorted back to front, and tonemapping into the 8-bit output. A forward renderer keeps materials, transparency and MSAA-free anti-aliasing options open, and needs one G-buffer target (normals), not four
- **glTF's metallic-roughness BRDF** - GGX, height-correlated Smith visibility, Schlick Fresnel and Lambert diffuse; base color and emissive textures sampled as sRGB, the rest linear; alpha modes opaque, mask (alpha-tested in every pass) and blend; double-sided materials; mirrored instances get pipelines with the winding flipped. Shader permutations are separate entry points, not defines (see [Decision 0008](0008-shader-pipeline.md)); pipelines are created on first use
- **Three descriptor sets** - the view (frame constants, lights, ambient occlusion, shadow map; `space0`), the environment (prefiltered map, BRDF lookup table, sky; `space1`) and the material (constants, five textures and samplers; `space2`), with the per-draw world and normal matrices and light range in push constants. Each set changes at its own rate
- **Punctual lights per draw** - point and spot lights that can light the view are sorted by distance (at most 1024), and each draw gets the list of those whose range reaches its bounds (at most 64) through a light index buffer. Directional lights (at most 4) are in the frame constants
- **Reversed depth** - 1 at the near plane, 0 at the far plane, in a 32-bit float buffer, which spreads precision evenly over distance; the editor camera's 0.05 m to 5 km range needs it
- **Cascaded shadow maps with PCSS** - the first shadow-casting directional light gets 4 cascades (a `Texture2DArray`), each fit to a bounding sphere of its slice of the view and snapped to whole texels so edges don't shimmer, with a depth range reaching back to every caster. Lookups use normal offsets, slope-scaled bias, and percentage-closer soft shadows: a blocker search estimates the caster's distance, which sets the filter width, so shadows are sharp at contact and soften with distance. `RenderSettings.ShadowSoftness` sets the light's angular size. Spot and point lights don't cast shadows yet
- **Ground-truth ambient occlusion (GTAO)** - horizon search over the depth buffer in two slices per pixel, integrating cosine-weighted visibility analytically, rotated in a 4x4 pattern and blurred 4x4 bilaterally. It replaced a normal-oriented hemisphere kernel, whose occlusion faded within centimeters of a crease. It darkens only the environment's light, with specular occlusion approximated from it
- **Split-sum image-based lighting** - an HDRI becomes an RGBA16F cube map (compute: equirectangular to cube, then mips), prefiltered per roughness by GGX importance sampling that reads lower mips by sample density (filtered importance sampling, which keeps the sun from speckling); the diffuse light is order-2 spherical harmonics computed on the CPU, and the BRDF lookup table is computed on the CPU once per process. CPU parts are deterministic and unit-tested. Scenes without an environment get a plain grey sky. Equirectangular images look toward -Z at their center
- **Tonemapping** - exposure in stops, then ACES (Hill's fit), Khronos PBR Neutral or a clamp, then the sRGB transfer function, written to an 8-bit UNORM target the editor can sample and read back
- **Assets load on first use, synchronously** - `RenderAssets` asks a `RenderAssetSource` (the editor's asset registry, the benchmark's fixtures) for models and environments by UUID and uploads them in the frame's command list, generating mips on the CPU. Failures are logged once and remembered; missing environments fall back to the default. The editor reloads assets whose files changed after a scan
- **Per-view targets and stats** - each view (the viewport, screenshots, the benchmark) has a `SceneRenderTarget` with its own images, buffers and GPU timer queries; `RenderStats` reports draw calls and triangles per pass, meshes seen, lights, CPU recording time and per-pass GPU times (from the latest finished frame; none where the device can't time work - no timer queries, or timestamps that don't advance within a frame, as with MoltenVK without GPU counters)
- **Editor overlays are lines** - `LineRenderer` draws the grid, entity markers and the selection outline over the output, depth-tested against the scene's depth or on top. Picking is on the CPU, against mesh triangles (with markers for entities that draw nothing)

## Alternatives
- **Deferred shading** - cheaper with very many lights, but four render targets of bandwidth, no MSAA, and transparency still needs a forward path. Clustered forward would scale lights further and can replace per-draw lists later
- **Variance or moment shadow maps** - soft, but light bleeding and more memory; PCF-based PCSS is simpler and sharp at contact
- **The classic normal-oriented hemisphere SSAO** - implemented first and replaced: with the samples it can afford, occlusion is thin and faint
- **Spherical harmonics or prefiltering entirely on the GPU** - the SH projection is cheap on the CPU and testable there; prefiltering a cube on the CPU is too slow
- **Asynchronous asset streaming** - needed for large worlds; synchronous loading on first use is simple and correct for now

## Consequences
- Every feature has a reference image on lavapipe (`Tests/Render/SceneRendererTests.cpp`), with driver-independent checks on every platform. Image-based lighting is checked with generated environments and with three photographed CC0 HDRIs from Poly Haven (outdoors, a studio, a sunset), committed at 1k through Git LFS. The CPU parts (extraction, cascades, culling, SH, the BRDF table, mips) are unit tests in `LodestoneClientTests`
- The benchmark (`LodestoneBenchmark`) measures the frame on a real GPU against a recorded baseline; CI gates its draw calls and reports its CPU time (see [Testing](../Testing.md#performance))
- Loading a large model stalls the frame that first uses it. Shadows from point and spot lights, light clustering, MSAA or TAA, bloom, auto-exposure and skinned meshes (Milestone 6) are later additions; the passes and descriptor sets leave room for them
- Hot reload of textures and models is coarse: an asset reloads when its own file's time changes after a scan, not when a texture it refers to does
- Each draw's light list tests every visible punctual light against its bounds, which costs draws times lights on the CPU each frame - fine for the benchmark's dozens of lights, and what light clustering will replace when scenes need hundreds
- A loaded model keeps its CPU vertices and indices next to the GPU buffers, for the editor's picking and outlines. The runtime, which doesn't pick, can drop them once it has its own loading path (Milestone 12's packed assets)
